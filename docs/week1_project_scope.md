# Week 1 Project Scope — TalentRank

| | |
|---|---|
| **Project Name** | TalentRank — Explainable Machine Learning for Employee Promotion Prediction and Ranking |
| **Domain** | Human Resource Management (Talent Management) / Machine Learning / Explainable AI |
| **Problem Statement** | Promotion decisions in many organisations depend mainly on supervisors' judgements, and finding employees who qualify for promotion takes more time and effort as the organisation grows. Machine learning can learn patterns from historical HR records, but a prediction on its own does not show HR which recorded factors drove it, and an unchecked model may repeat past bias. TalentRank predicts historical promotion outcomes, ranks employees by predicted probability and explains each ranking, giving HR Talent Management Specialists and top management additional information for human review. It does not make promotion decisions. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** Current promotion processes in most organisations depend on supervisors' judgements, and manually identifying employees qualified for promotion takes more time as the company grows; machine learning on historical HR records (54,808 employees) was proposed to support this task.<br>Source / citation: Alqahtani, F. A., & Almaleh, A. (2022). Analysis and prediction of employee promotions using machine learning. *DSIT 2022*, IEEE. https://doi.org/10.1109/DSIT55514.2022.9943959<br><br>**Evidence 2:** AI systems that influence promotion decisions are classified as high-risk under the EU AI Act (Annex III, point 4), requiring transparency, human oversight and bias control. A promotion model therefore needs explanations and fairness checks, not prediction alone.<br>Source / citation: European Commission AI Act Service Desk — Employment. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/node/464 |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | **Study / solution 1:** Alqahtani & Almaleh (2022) compare seven classifiers (KNN, Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM, AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting) with SMOTE on 54,808 records. Gradient Boosting performs best (AUC 0.79); previous-year rating is the most important feature.<br>Source / citation: https://doi.org/10.1109/DSIT55514.2022.9943959<br>Key limitation: Evaluates classification only — no ranked list of employees and no explanation for individual employees. Gender appears among the most important features, but no fairness analysis is done.<br><br>**Study / solution 2:** Bhattacharya et al. (2023) balance the data with SMOTE-ENN, train five classifiers (XGBoost best, 95% accuracy) and explain predictions with LIME and SHAP. Top features: previous-year rating, average training score, length of service.<br>Source / citation: Bhattacharya, A., Choudhary, P., Mukhopadhyay, S., Misra, B., Chakraborty, S., & Dey, N. (2023). Explainable AI for predictive analytics on employee promotion. *ICACTA 2023*, IEEE. https://doi.org/10.1109/ICACTA58201.2023.10393141<br>Key limitation: Reports accuracy, which is misleading when only 8.5% of employees are promoted. Treats SHAP features as the organisation's real reasons for promotion, which explanations cannot prove. Uses gender and age as inputs without a fairness check. No ranking.<br><br>**Study / solution 3:** Benabou & Touhami (2026) compare eight models, applying SMOTE only inside cross-validation folds; Random Forest reaches 96.21% accuracy. Previous-year rating and training score are key features.<br>Source / citation: Benabou, A., & Touhami, F. (2026). Optimizing employee promotion predictions using machine learning. *International Journal of Production Management and Engineering, 14*(1), 16–33. https://doi.org/10.4995/ijpme.2026.23364<br>Key limitation: Global feature importance only — no individual explanations, no ranking evaluation and no fairness audit. The authors list demographics and ethical considerations as future work.<br><br>**Study / solution 4:** Bitkuri et al. (2025) review machine learning ranking systems: pointwise, pairwise and listwise learning-to-rank, ranking metrics such as precision, and fairness risks in critical domains such as hiring.<br>Source / citation: Bitkuri, V., Kendyala, R., Madhavaram, C. R., Gollangi, H. K., & Bauskar, S. R. (2025). A review of machine learning ranking systems: Methods, applications, and challenges. *Journal of Contemporary Education Theory & Artificial Intelligence, 4*(1), 100119. https://doi.org/10.47991/2996-4954/JCETAI-119<br>Key limitation: General review (search, e-commerce, advertising); it does not apply ranking or ranking fairness to HR promotion data. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | What practical or technical limitation remains unresolved?<br>Gap / Limitation: Studies 1–3 use the same dataset and report 94–96% accuracy, which is only slightly better than the 91.5% obtained by predicting "not promoted" for every employee. None of them evaluates the **ranked list** that HR would actually review (Study 4 shows rankings need ranking metrics), none **audits fairness** across employee groups, and Study 2 presents explanations as decision reasons. TalentRank combines prediction, ranking, explanation with stated limits, and a fairness check in one decision-support workflow. This is an engineering gap, not a claim of scientific novelty. |
| **Initial Project Objectives** | **O1:** To investigate employee features associated with historical promotion outcomes in a public HR dataset.<br>**O2:** To develop explainable machine learning models that predict historical promotion outcomes, rank employees by predicted probability and explain each ranking.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the developed models in terms of predictive performance, ranking quality, explanation stability and fairness across employee groups. |
| **Target Users** | **HR Talent Management Specialists** — review ranked employees and the features influencing each prediction.<br>**Top Management** — obtain additional, explained information when reviewing employees for promotion.<br>Intended use: additional information for human review and discussion, covering all nine departments in the dataset. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1:** Employees Evaluation for Promotion (Kaggle) — 54,808 employee records, 13 columns, historical promotion recommendations — URL: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/muhammadimran112233/employees-evaluation-for-promotion<br><br>**Source 2:** WNS Analytics Wizard 2018 HR Analytics challenge (Analytics Vidhya) — original problem statement and variable definitions — URL: https://datahack.analyticsvidhya.com/contest/wns-analytics-hackathon-2018-1/<br><br>**Data note:** the target `is_promoted` means "recommended for promotion" in a past promotion cycle. It does not measure leadership potential. |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both use AI to give users better information for a decision, make outputs understandable to their users, and act as decision support rather than replacing human judgement. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | EduAdvisor uses retrieval-augmented generation over university documents. TalentRank uses structured HR records and supervised machine learning to predict, rank and explain historical promotion outcomes. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | **Risk 1 — Bias and fairness:** historical promotion records may contain organisational bias; a model can learn it and rank some groups (e.g., by gender or age) lower. Harmed: employees in those groups.<br><br>**Risk 2 — Privacy:** employee records hold personal attributes; exposing them would harm employees and breach data-protection obligations such as Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act 2010.<br><br>**Risk 3 — Over-reliance:** HR or management may treat a ranking as a final recommendation. Harmed: employees affected by unreviewed decisions. Results must show their limits, and final decisions stay with humans. |
