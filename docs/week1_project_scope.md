# Week 1 Project Scope — TalentRank: Explainable Machine Learning for Employee Promotion Prediction and Ranking

| | |
|---|---|
| **Project Name** | TalentRank: Explainable Machine Learning for Employee Promotion Prediction and Ranking |
| **Domain** | Human Resource Management (Talent Management) / Machine Learning / Explainable AI |
| **Problem Statement** | In many organisations, promotion decisions depend mainly on supervisors' judgements, and finding the employees who qualify for promotion takes more time and effort as the organisation grows. Machine learning can learn patterns from historical HR records and apply them consistently to every employee, which a manual review or a fixed rule cannot easily do across thousands of records. However, a prediction alone does not show HR which recorded factors drove it, and an unchecked model may repeat past bias. TalentRank predicts historical promotion outcomes, ranks employees by predicted probability and explains each ranking, giving HR Talent Management Specialists and top management additional information for human review. It does not make promotion decisions. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** The current promotion process in most organisations depends on supervisors' judgements, and the larger the company, the more time and effort it takes to identify employees qualified for promotion manually.<br>Source / citation: Alqahtani, F. A., & Almaleh, A. (2022). Analysis and prediction of employee promotions using machine learning. *2022 5th International Conference on Data Science and Information Technology (DSIT)*, IEEE. https://doi.org/10.1109/DSIT55514.2022.9943959<br><br>**Evidence 2:** AI systems used to make or support decisions that affect promotion are classified as high-risk under the EU AI Act (Annex III, point 4). Such systems need transparency, human oversight and bias control, so a promotion model needs explanations and fairness checks, not only predictions.<br>Source / citation: European Commission, AI Act Service Desk — Annex III: Employment, workers' management and access to self-employment. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/node/464 |

Gradient Boosting performs best (AUC 79.28%), and previous-year rating is the most important feature.<br>Source / citation: https://doi.org/10.1109/DSIT55514.2022.9943959<br>Key limitation: Classification only, with no ranked list of employees and no explanation for each employee. Gender appears among the most important features and the authors suggest organisations emphasise these features, yet no fairness test is carried out.<br><br>**Study / solution 2:** Bhattacharya et al. (2023) balance the data with SMOTE-ENN, train five classifiers (XGBoost best, 95% accuracy) and explain predictions with SHAP and LIME. The top features are previous-year rating, average training score and length of service.<br>Source / citation: Bhattacharya, A., Choudhary, P., Mukhopadhyay, S., Misra, B., Chakraborty, S., & Dey, N. (2023). Explainable AI for predictive analytics on employee promotion. *2023 International Conference on Advanced Computing Technologies and Applications (ICACTA)*, IEEE. https://doi.org/10.1109/ICACTA58201.2023.10393141<br>Key limitation: Models are judged by accuracy, which is misleading when only 8.5% of employees are promoted. SMOTE-ENN is reported on the whole dataset, so the score may be optimistic. SHAP features are described as the organisation's reasons for promotion, although explanations only show how the model behaves. Gender and age are model inputs with no fairness check, and there is no ranking.<br><br>**Study / solution 3:** Benabou & Touhami (2026) compare eight models using stratified 5-fold cross-validation, applying SMOTE only inside the training folds. Random Forest performs best (96.21% accuracy, AUC-ROC 0.99), and previous-year rating and average training score are key features.<br>Source / citation: Benabou, A., & Touhami, F. (2026). The authors list fairness auditing and validation on other time periods as future work.<br><br>**Study / solution 4:** Bitkuri et al. (2025) review machine learning ranking systems. In pointwise ranking, a binary classifier scores each candidate individually and candidates are sorted by that score. Ranked lists are evaluated with ranking metrics such as precision, and fairness is a concern in critical domains such as hiring.<br>Source / citation: Bitkuri, V., Kendyala, R., Madhavaram, C. It does not apply ranking or ranking fairness to HR promotion data. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | What practical or technical limitation remains unresolved?<br>Gap / Limitation: Studies 1–3 use the same dataset and report 94–96% accuracy, which is only slightly better than the 91.5% obtained by predicting "not promoted" for every employee. None of them evaluates the **ranked list** that HR would actually review (Study 4 shows that rankings need ranking metrics), none **audits fairness** across employee groups, and Study 2 presents model explanations as real decision reasons. TalentRank combines prediction, ranking, explanation with stated limits, and a fairness check in one decision-support workflow. This is an engineering gap, not a claim of scientific novelty. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2–3)** | **O1:** To investigate employee features associated with historical promotion outcomes in a public HR dataset.<br>**O2:** To develop explainable machine learning models that predict historical promotion outcomes, rank employees by predicted probability and explain each ranking.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the developed models in terms of predictive performance, ranking quality, explanation stability and fairness across employee groups. |
| **Target Users** | **HR Talent Management Specialists** — review ranked employees and the features influencing each prediction.<br>**Top Management** — obtain additional, explained information when reviewing employees for promotion.<br>Intended use: additional information for human review and discussion across all nine departments in the dataset. Final promotion decisions remain with HR and top management. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1:** Employees Evaluation for Promotion (Kaggle) — 54,808 employee records, 13 columns, nine departments, historical promotion recommendations (`employee_promotion.csv`) — URL: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/muhammadimran112233/employees-evaluation-for-promotion<br><br>**Source 2:** WNS Analytics Wizard 2018 — HR Analytics challenge (Analytics Vidhya) — original problem statement and variable definitions for the same data — URL: https://datahack.analyticsvidhya.com/contest/wns-analytics-hackathon-2018-1/<br><br>**Data note:** the target `is_promoted` means "recommended for promotion" in a past promotion cycle. It does not measure merit or leadership potential. |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both use AI to give users better information for a decision, make outputs understandable to their users, and act as decision support rather than replacing human judgement. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | EduAdvisor uses retrieval-augmented generation over university documents to answer questions. TalentRank uses structured HR records and supervised machine learning to predict, rank and explain historical promotion outcomes. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | **Risk 1 — Bias and fairness:** historical promotion records may contain organisational bias → the model learns it and ranks some groups (e.g., by gender or age) lower → employees in those groups are harmed.<br>Planned control: gender and age are not used as model inputs; they are used only to audit the ranked list for fairness.<br><br>**Risk 2 — Privacy:** employee records hold personal attributes → exposing them would harm employees and breach data-protection obligations such as Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act 2010.<br>Planned control: only the public, anonymised dataset is used; no real company employee data enters the repository.<br><br>**Risk 3 — Over-reliance:** HR or management treat the ranking as a final decision → employees are affected by unreviewed decisions.<br>Planned control: every output is labelled as decision support and shows its limits; final decisions stay with humans. |

## Scope Constraints

The Topic 1 lab advises avoiding autonomous decision-making unless the scope is carefully constrained. TalentRank is constrained as follows:

- **Decision support only:** the system ranks and explains. It never promotes or rejects anyone.
- **Historical target:** predictions describe similarity to past promotion recommendations, not merit, potential or the position an employee should move into.
- **Explanations show model behaviour:** feature contributions explain the model's output, not the organisation's actual reasons for a promotion.
- **Public data only:** the project uses a public, anonymised dataset; no real employee records are used.
